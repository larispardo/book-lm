How decoding works
  1. Logits. The model scores every one of ~49k tokens. Softmax turns those scores into the raw probabilities: what the model actually believes.
  2. Repetition penalty. Tokens already in the context get their logit divided (if positive) or multiplied (if negative) by the penalty, so repeats become less likely.
  3. Temperature. Logits are divided by T. T<1 sharpens the distribution toward the favourite, T>1 flattens it, T=0 means greedy: always take the top token. The result is the scaled distribution.
  4. Top-k. Keep only the k highest-scoring tokens.
  5. Top-p (nucleus). Keep the smallest set of top tokens whose probability adds up to p.
  6. Min-p. Keep tokens at least min-p × as likely as the favourite. This adapts: strict when the model is confident, loose when it is not.
  7. Renormalise. Survivors' probabilities are rescaled to sum to 1. This is the final distribution.
  8. Sample. Draw one token from it, append it to the text, and repeat.

Click any generated token to see these three distributions side by side, then move a slider and watch them change.

Models to compare

All models share one tokenizer (SmolLM2 byte-level BPE), so their token-level predictions line up. Validation loss is cross-entropy in nats per token on held-out Holmes text; perplexity = eloss (“as unsure as choosing uniformly among this many tokens”). Lower is better.
